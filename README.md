## The review (111 characters)

> One star. I give it eight hours every night and wake up tired anyway. At this point I am just renting darkness.

A deadpan one-star review of sleep. The grievance is the sharpest true
one sleep has: eight hours in, still tired in the morning. The turn lands
in the last few words — "renting darkness" reframes the whole complaint
as a bad deal, with no winking and no exclamation mark doing the work.

## How it runs

`main.py` (stdlib only) writes the review to `/output/entry.txt`
(or `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` when set) and self-validates the
gate: UTF-8, one line, 1–160 characters.

```sh
python3 main.py
```

## The 10 candidates

Chosen after a freshness search to avoid published stock lines (the
"sun was too bright" restaurant review, the "only one star" sun pun,
the "impossible to put down" anti-gravity book joke, and the usual
sun/gravity puns). Every candidate is one line, deadpan, no exclamation
marks, and within the 160-character limit.

1. **Sun, 124 chars** — "One star. It rises at 5am, shines directly into my eyes through a gap in the curtains, and has never once apologised for it." Specific daily annoyance (the curtain gap), but the "never apologised" turn is weaker than the winner's.
2. **Gravity, 109 chars** — "One star. It makes going upstairs hard work and going downstairs dangerous, and it charges the same for both." Good deadpan pricing turn, but the grievance is less sharp than sleep's.
3. **Sleep, 111 chars — CHOSEN** — "One star. I give it eight hours every night and wake up tired anyway. At this point I am just renting darkness." Sharpest true grievance, tightest turn.
4. **Water, 107 chars** — "One star. No taste, no colour, no smell, and I am still expected to drink eight glasses of it a day or die." Sharp and tight, but the "or die" ending is a punchline more than a turn.
5. **Oxygen, 119 chars** — "One star. I have to keep taking it in every few seconds or I die, and it has never once offered a discount for loyalty." Funny loyalty angle, but the grievance is the dependency itself, which is thinner than sleep's broken promise.
6. **Mondays, 128 chars** — "One star. It shows up every single week without being invited, does nothing useful, and somehow always feels longer than Friday." Relatable, but "longer than Friday" is a comparison, not a sting.
7. **Sun, 109 chars** — "One star. It burns my skin, fades my furniture, and then hides behind a cloud the one day I actually want it." Good turn, but two grievances crowd one joke.
8. **Sleep, 124 chars** — "One star. It takes a third of my life, gives me dreams about being late for exams I finished years ago, and calls that rest." Strong runner-up; the exam-dream detail is specific, but the winner's turn is tighter.
9. **Water, 128 chars** — "One star. It falls from the sky without warning, ruins my hair, soaks my shoes, and then has the nerve to call itself essential." Good "call itself essential" turn, but three grievances in one line.
10. **Mondays, 119 chars** — "One star. It is the only day with its own alarm clock, and it still expects me to be grateful for the weekend it ended." Nice "weekend it ended" turn, but the alarm-clock premise is weaker than sleep's.
